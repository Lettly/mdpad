# Math

## Inline

The mass–energy relation is $E = mc^2$, and the area of a circle is $A = \pi r^2$.
Euler's identity $e^{i\pi} + 1 = 0$ is short enough to sit in a sentence.

## Block

$$
\int_0^1 x^2 \, dx = \frac{1}{3}
$$

$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n^2} = \frac{\pi^2}{6}
$$

## Aligned equations

$$
\begin{aligned}
(a + b)^2 &= a^2 + 2ab + b^2 \\
(a - b)^2 &= a^2 - 2ab + b^2 \\
a^2 - b^2 &= (a + b)(a - b)
\end{aligned}
$$

## In context

When a derivation is long, it helps to keep the prose and the formulas together:

1. Start from the definition: $F = ma$.
2. Substitute the acceleration $a = \frac{dv}{dt}$.
3. Integrate over time to obtain the impulse:

$$
\int_{t_0}^{t_1} F \, dt = m v_1 - m v_0
$$

> A quote can carry a formula as well: $\Delta E = h\nu$.

## Bracket syntax

Display maths also works with brackets:

\[
\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin x}{x} = 1
\]
